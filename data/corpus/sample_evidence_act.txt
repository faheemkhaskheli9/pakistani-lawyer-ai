Sample Evidence Act (Illustrative Demo Text)

This document is an original, fictional sample authored for this project
to illustrate how a general law of evidence might be structured. It does
not reproduce, paraphrase, or derive from any specific real statute of
Pakistan or any other jurisdiction, and must not be relied on as legal
authority or legal advice.

Section 1. Relevancy of Facts
Facts which, though not in issue, are so connected with a fact in issue
as to form part of the same transaction are relevant, whether they
occurred at the same time and place or at different times and places.

Section 2. Admissions
An admission is a statement, oral or documentary, which suggests any
inference as to a fact in issue or relevant fact, made by a party to the
proceeding or a person with an interest in its subject matter.

Section 3. Dying Declaration
A statement made by a person as to the cause of that person's death, or
as to any circumstance of the transaction which resulted in death, is
relevant in a case in which the cause of that person's death comes into
question, whether or not the person was under expectation of death when
the statement was made.

Section 4. Burden of Proof
The burden of proving a fact rests on the party who asserts the
affirmative of the issue, and not on the party who denies it, except
where a specific law otherwise provides that the burden lies elsewhere.
